# Ready-to-import Jinni demo libraries

Three English packs, with three small source files each. **Every file is fictional, AI-generated test data.** Separate files play distinct source roles; none claims independent real-world authorship, verification, live prices or official policy. Each pack deliberately contains a cross-source conflict so Jinni can expose uncertainty rather than hide it. Packs are file groups to select within the existing library, not separate notebooks.

## Import and use

1. In desktop Jinni, open **Notes & files** and import the three `.md` files from one pack. Mark each source fictional/AI-generated (simulated). Do not import this README as evidence.
2. In **Conditions & sources**, select **only those three sources**. Deselect other library items. Choose **Your own idea** if you want to avoid presenting an unrelated preset as the pack's calculation.
3. Open **AI settings**, select your provider/model and save its key on this device, or use configured Vertex ADC. Review the recipient notice. This guide does not configure credentials or make a provider call.
4. Copy the pack's **Goal** below into the goal field and choose **Suggest options**. Then copy its **Ask AI** question into the separate question panel.
5. Open returned citations and compare their exact quotes with the imported files. Expect two or three proposals, trade-offs and missing information; provider wording/order can vary. A verified quote is not proof that the underlying fictional claim is true.

The imported notes/files inform **AI proposals and answers**. They do **not** automatically extract measurements, modify limits/weights, populate a custom dataset or alter the deterministic comparison. If a preset comparison is included, its calculated result stays separate from these pack choices. Browser Jinni can import/search these files locally, but cloud AI proposals require desktop. For an absent-answer question, look for an explicit evidence gap rather than an invented fact; a matching but incomplete passage can still trigger a provider request.

## 1. Weekend day trip

Import [venue catalog](weekend-day-trip/01-venue-catalog.md), [travel desk](weekend-day-trip/02-travel-desk.md) and [personal priorities](weekend-day-trip/03-personal-priorities.md).

**Goal**

> Suggest two or three weekend day trip choices from Lantern Museum, Meadow Park and Maker Studio. Use only the selected sources. Budget GBP 35 including admission, return transport and the GBP 8 meal allowance. Leave no earlier than 09:00 and return by 18:00 on 24 October 2026, Europe/London. Prefer indoors. Cite facts from the venue, travel and priorities notes, explain the museum price conflict and flag unconfirmed access or bookings. Keep these proposals separate from preset scores.

**Ask AI**

> For this weekend day trip, compare the total costs and return times of Lantern Museum, Meadow Park and Maker Studio. Which facts conflict, and what would I need to confirm before choosing? Quote the selected sources.

**Expected facts and uncertainties**

- Lantern Museum: GBP 32 or GBP 34 including the meal allowance, depending on the conflicting GBP 8/10 admission claim. Arrival 09:45 precedes its 10:00 opening; home 17:15. It is indoors.
- Meadow Park: GBP 18 including the meal allowance, home 17:00, outdoors with unknown weather/shelter suitability.
- Maker Studio: GBP 34 including the meal allowance, home 17:05, indoors; booking and step-free access are unconfirmed.
- All three fit the stated estimated budget/time on the supplied fictional information, but no source verifies real service availability, meal price or access.

**Absent-answer question**

> What is the actual weather forecast for Meadow Park on 24 October 2026?

No forecast is supplied. An answer must acknowledge the gap.

## 2. Laptop choice

Import [product cards](laptop-choice/01-product-cards.md), [warranty desk](laptop-choice/02-warranty-desk.md) and [study needs](laptop-choice/03-study-needs.md).

**Goal**

> Compare Alder 13, Birch 15 and Cedar 14 for Python coursework and web development. Suggest two supported choices and a conditional alternative if useful. Budget GBP 750 including two years of warranty; delivery charges are unknown. Prefer 16 GB RAM, low weight and longer battery life, without treating preferences as hard limits. Cite the selected product, warranty and needs notes. Explain the Cedar warranty conflict rather than assuming the cheaper policy.

**Ask AI**

> Which laptop choices have confirmed two-year totals below GBP 750 in these fictional sources? Compare memory and weight, and explain why Cedar 14 remains uncertain. Cite exact quotes.

**Expected facts and uncertainties**

- Alder 13: GBP 719 including two-year coverage, 8 GB non-upgradeable RAM, 1.2 kg. Light and affordable, with a memory trade-off.
- Birch 15: GBP 739 including two-year coverage, 16 GB RAM, 1.8 kg. More memory, heavier.
- Cedar 14: GBP 749 if the product card's included two-year cover applies, or GBP 829 under the warranty desk's policy. Two-year eligibility within budget is unresolved; it is not a confirmed feasible purchase.
- Battery figures are advertising claims, not measurements. Delivery, stock and Linux compatibility are unknown.

**Absent-answer question**

> What is Alder 13's independently measured battery life while running a Python IDE for eight hours?

There is no measured battery test.

## 3. Campus learning opportunities

Import [opportunity catalog](campus-learning/01-opportunity-catalog.md), [eligibility desk](campus-learning/02-eligibility-desk.md) and [learning goals](campus-learning/03-learning-goals.md).

**Goal**

> Suggest one campus learning opportunity and two alternatives from OpenCode Lab, Data Clinic and Research Sprint. I am a first-year learner with basic Python but no completed Python module. My session-fee budget is GBP 25 and I am available 09:00–17:00 on 24 October 2026, Europe/London. Prefer practical Python and data skills. Use the three selected sources, check full-session timing and eligibility, flag the Data Clinic contradiction, and quote evidence for each option. Do not treat uncertain eligibility as confirmed.

**Ask AI**

> Why might OpenCode Lab suit this learner better than Research Sprint, and what conflicting evidence prevents a definite Data Clinic recommendation? Cite the goals, catalog and eligibility notes.

**Expected facts and uncertainties**

- OpenCode Lab: GBP 0, 10:00–12:00, first-year/basic Python accepted, laptop required and owned. It directly supports practical coding.
- Research Sprint: GBP 20, 15:00–17:00, first-year/individual sign-up accepted. It meets the time boundary but emphasizes research pitches and speaking rather than Python.
- Data Clinic: GBP 15, 13:00–15:00. The catalog says no Python experience is needed; the eligibility desk requires a completed module. This learner has not completed one, so eligibility needs clarification.
- All fees are within GBP 25; that does not establish travel/meal costs. Places and accessibility are unknown. The output can include Data Clinic only as a clearly conditional alternative.

**Absent-answer question**

> How many confirmed seats remain in OpenCode Lab right now?

Remaining seats are not supplied and these are not live university listings.
